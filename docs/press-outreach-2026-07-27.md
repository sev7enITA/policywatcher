# PolicyWatcher press outreach plan — 27 July 2026

## Campaign position

PolicyWatcher should not be pitched as another AI compliance score or as a tool that certifies companies. The strongest editorial story is this:

> Public policies change continuously, but the path from a notification to a verifiable source is usually opaque. PolicyWatcher is an open-source civic-tech project that keeps source evidence, timestamps, limitations, missing data and human review visible alongside AI-assisted interpretation.

The immediate news hook is the EU AI Act transparency timetable: the European Commission says the Article 50 transparency obligations apply from 2 August 2026. PolicyWatcher does not certify compliance, but it offers a timely, inspectable example of how public-facing transparency, provenance and data-quality boundaries can be implemented in software. See the [European Commission guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-deployers-certain-ai-systems).

## Claims that are safe to make

- Open-source public-interest project, created and maintained by Fabrizio Degni.
- Configured inventory of 16 companies across six sectors; configured inventory is not the same as complete public evidence coverage.
- Fifteen canonical governance indicators spanning privacy, AI governance and ethics.
- Public outputs remain gated by retrieved source evidence; a missing assessment is shown as `Not assessed`, never converted into zero or a reassuring score.
- Source provenance, retrieval status, timestamps, limitations and data-quality context remain visible across public analysis and exports.
- Regional EU, US and Global perspectives and Individual/Enterprise views are supported when evidence is available.
- English and Italian interface, accessible chart summaries and exact-value tables.
- Browser companion remains a separately versioned Beta package with store submission planned; it is not presented as generally available in browser stores.

Do not claim legal advice, compliance certification, exhaustive monitoring, real-time coverage, vulnerability-free software, or verified correctness of every AI interpretation.

## Launch channels that can amplify coverage

These are not press outlets and must not be treated as mailing-list targets.

- **Product Hunt**: launch only after the new production package is online and every public link works. The [official launch guide](https://www.producthunt.com/launch) says makers can submit their own product and must not ask people directly for upvotes. Ask the community to try the product and leave substantive feedback.
- **Hacker News / Show HN**: suitable because the public dashboard can be tried without signup. Follow the [Show HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html), use a plain title, stay available for technical questions and never solicit votes or coordinated comments.
- **LinkedIn**: publish a native post from Fabrizio's profile with one strong visual and a short screen recording. Tag only people who are directly relevant or already involved; do not mass-tag journalists.
- **Existing supporters**: privately offer the updated demo to Tom's Hardware Italia, Massimo Chiriatti, Michele Iaselli and Giovanna Panucci before the public launch. Ask whether they find the update worth discussing, not for a forced repost.
- **Reddit**: do not post promotional copy in `r/privacy`; its moderators currently remove self-promotion. Use only communities whose current rules explicitly allow creator disclosures and technical project discussions.

Suggested Show HN title:

> Show HN: PolicyWatcher – evidence-first monitoring for public policy changes

Suggested Product Hunt tagline:

> Inspect what changed in digital policies, with sources, provenance and data-quality limits attached.

## Correct outreach sequence

1. **Production gate**: deploy the 27 July package first. Test homepage, What Changed, Timeline, Leaderboard, Trust, Methodology, Infographics, Feature Atlas and mobile navigation on the public domain.
2. **Press-kit gate**: publish one stable page containing a 60-word description, fact sheet, founder bio/headshot, logo files, four screenshots, 45–60 second demo, release notes, methodology, Trust links and contact address.
3. **One exclusive, if desired**: give one top-tier Italian publication 48 hours to accept a specific exclusive case study. Do not offer the same exclusive simultaneously elsewhere.
4. **Italian wave**: send six individually personalized A-priority messages between 08:00 and 10:00 Europe/Rome on Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday. Send B-priority messages the following day.
5. **International wave**: use the English pitch, but rewrite the first paragraph for each beat. Contributor outlets receive proposals or exclusive drafts according to their rules, never the generic release.
6. **Community launch**: Product Hunt, Show HN and LinkedIn should go live only after journalists have had a reasonable early look and the demo is stable.
7. **One follow-up**: after four or five business days, reply in the same thread with one new fact, screenshot or reproducible case. If there is no reply, stop.
8. **Coverage loop**: add earned coverage to the Press Wall, thank the author, publish a concise correction if any claim changes, and give every article a distinct share card.

## Campaign measurement

“Viral” cannot be guaranteed. The controllable objective is a compounding evidence loop. Track:

- personalized pitches sent and delivery failures;
- replies, demo requests and interviews;
- earned articles and newsletter inclusions;
- referral sessions by publication;
- demo completion, methodology visits and GitHub stars/forks;
- Product Hunt/Hacker News comments and qualitative objections;
- citations from privacy, governance and open-source communities;
- corrections requested and time to resolve them.

Use campaign parameters only on public campaign links and never include personal recipient information in URLs.
